@circle IELTS Reading
@ielts Gardens in the sky
Paragraph A. Twenty years ago, a rooftop covered in lush vegetation would have seemed eccentric. Today, in cities from Singapore to Copenhagen, green roofs are increasingly seen as a practical response to monumental urban problems. They cool buildings in summer, reduce energy bills and help to mitigate the impact of sudden storms by holding rainwater.
Paragraph B. Yet the spread of green roofs has not been even. In Copenhagen, a law passed in 2010 made green roofs compulsory on most new buildings with flat roofs. In many other cities, however, owners are simply encouraged, and progress has been much slower.
Paragraph C. The benefits are not only environmental. A study of office workers found that those who could see foliage from their desks reported lower stress levels. Some hospitals have created rooftop gardens where patients can spend time outdoors, and doctors say this can tackle feelings of isolation.
Paragraph D. Critics point out that green roofs are expensive to build and maintain, and that older buildings may not be strong enough to support the weight of soil and water. For this reason, supporters argue that the most radical changes should begin with new construction.
@match Which paragraph contains the following information?

benefits for people's mental health = Paragraph C ~ reported lower stress levels
practical difficulties with older buildings = Paragraph D ~ older buildings may not be strong enough
a list of environmental advantages = Paragraph A ~ They cool buildings in summer
- Paragraph E
@endmatch

@short Office workers who could see plants reported lower ___ levels. = stress ~ reported lower stress levels
@tfng Copenhagen's law applies to all buildings in the city. = false ~ on most new buildings with flat roofs
@tfng Green roofs are cheaper to maintain than ordinary roofs. = not given
@endielts

@circle IELTS Reading
@ielts The blue roof experiment
Paragraph A. When a district in Rotterdam suffered three serious floods in five years, local engineers decided to try something radical. Instead of draining rainwater away as quickly as possible, they would keep it on the rooftops. The idea, known as the "blue roof", is to store water for hours or even days and release it slowly.
Paragraph B. The first blue roofs were installed on new buildings, which can carry greater weight. Retro-fitting older blocks proved more difficult and expensive, and in some cases caused weeks of disruption for residents.
Paragraph C. After three years, the results were encouraging. Flooding in the district fell sharply, and stored water was used to irrigate nearby parks during dry summers. Officials now hope the scheme will become mainstream across the country.
@tfng The engineers' idea was to remove rainwater from roofs faster. = false ~ Instead of draining rainwater away as quickly as possible
@tfng Older buildings were easier to adapt than new ones. = false ~ Retro-fitting older blocks proved more difficult and expensive
@tfng Some residents complained about the noise of the works. = not given

* that the scheme will be used all over the country
- that the district will never flood again
- that residents will pay for the roofs

@circle IELTS Reading
@ielts Praise and performance
Paragraph A. For most of the twentieth century, teachers assumed that intelligence was largely innate: some children were simply clever, and others were not. That assumption began to lose ground in the 1970s, as researchers showed that cognitive skills could be improved through practice.
Paragraph B. In a recent study, a group of 200 teenagers completed a difficult maths task. Half were told afterwards that they must be "naturally gifted"; the other half were praised for their effort and strategies. When offered a second, harder task, most of the first group chose an easier option, while most of the second group accepted the challenge.
Paragraph C. The researcher, Dr Lena Ortiz, believes the results show that praising talent can make students afraid of failure. However, Professor Mark Hale, who reviewed the study, warns that it was carried out in a single school and that the effect may not last more than a few weeks.
@tfng The belief that intelligence is innate was common for most of the twentieth century. = true ~ teachers assumed that intelligence was largely innate

@tfng Most students praised for effort accepted the harder task. = true ~ most of the second group accepted the challenge
@tfng The students' parents were also interviewed. = not given
@match Match each statement with the correct person.

@circle IELTS Reading
@ielts Can confidence be taught?
Paragraph A. Few ideas in education have spread as fast as the claim that confidence can be taught. Its proponents argue that if students believe they can improve, they will work harder and achieve more. Critics, however, warn against treating any single idea as an elixir.
Paragraph B. A two-year project in 40 secondary schools tested a programme of weekly confidence lessons. Students' self-reported confidence rose, but their exam results showed no significant change. The researchers concluded that the lessons may have been too direct: many students saw them as a sign that teachers doubted their ability.
Paragraph C. The schools that did see an improvement had taken a more subtle approach. Instead of lessons about confidence, teachers focused on specific skills and gave detailed feedback on effort. In the writer's view, this is a far more promising route, although it is harder to quantify.

@tfng Most teachers in the project wanted to continue the lessons. = not given
