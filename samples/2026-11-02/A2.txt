Lucy Grant didn't plan to be a writer. After university she became a nurse in a big hospital in Leeds. She loved her job, but in the evenings she often felt tired and lonely.

@reading Why read for pleasure?
A lot of research shows that reading for pleasure is really important for young people. When you read a book because you want to, and not because a teacher says so, your brain gets many benefits.
First, reading improves your vocabulary and your grammar. It also helps you to spell correctly and to write well. Readers often understand other people better, because in books they see the world through other people's eyes.
Reading can also stop people feeling lonely, and it can be relaxing. Some people prefer novels, others prefer non-fiction, like history books.
But there is a problem. A recent study found that many teenagers don't want their friends to see them reading a book. They think reading isn't cool. Maybe it's time to stop worrying about that and start enjoying a good book.
